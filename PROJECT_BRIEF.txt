ANIMAS RIVER AFTER GOLD KING
Did the 2015 Gold King Mine release leave a lasting, flow-adjusted signal?
Project brief for an autonomous implementation agent

PURPOSE

Ethan Levy is pursuing a data science role at a minerals company. His background includes geology/physics/mathematics, a master's in hydrology and water quality, environmental data work, production fraud-detection ML, and work since January 2025 as the founding engineer at a three-person AI startup. His portfolio has a reference groundwater analysis with an LLM evaluation, a verification harness for analysis agents (cairn), two statistics cheat sheets, and (in progress alongside this) a mineral prospectivity model. This project is the mine-water piece: an interrupted time series on a well-known event where the obvious answer is probably wrong.

The event: on 5 August 2015 an EPA-contracted crew breached the Gold King Mine adit near Silverton, Colorado, releasing about three million gallons of acidic, metal-laden water into Cement Creek, which flows into the Animas River and on to the San Juan. The plume passed within days. The central question is not whether the plume happened; it is: after adjusting for discharge and season, did metal concentrations in Cement Creek and the Animas River change in a lasting way after August 2015, and can any change be attributed to the release rather than to what came after it?

Do not decide the answer in advance. Plausible findings include: no lasting change; a decrease because the interim water treatment plant at Gladstone began treating Gold King discharge in autumn 2015; a change that depends entirely on which pre-period is chosen; or a change that is only detectable in some constituents at some sites. Any of these is a valid result. The portfolio's value is in exposing how the answer depends on the choices and in making those choices explicit.

KNOWN CONFOUNDERS TO EXPOSE, NOT ASSUME

Verify each against published sources before relying on it, and cite what you find in docs/decisions.md:

- The Gladstone interim water treatment plant began operating in October 2015 and treats Gold King discharge before it reaches Cement Creek.
- Bulkheads installed in the Sunnyside Mine workings and the American Tunnel between about 1996 and 2003 are associated with rising metal loads in Cement Creek through the 2000s, so the pre-period may contain a trend.
- The Bonita Peak Mining District was listed as a Superfund site in 2016, with other source-control work since.
- Sampling intensity rose sharply after the release, and detection limits, methods and sampling organisations changed with it. More samples at lower limits is not a concentration change.
- Spring snowmelt flushes stored metals; the plume itself was a few days. "Lasting" must be defined on a scale of months to years and the plume-passage window handled explicitly.

MINIMUM ANALYSIS

Pre-register (see cairn below) the sites, constituents, periods, model forms and decision rule before fitting.

1. Coverage: samples per site, constituent and year; organisations; detection limits over time; QC records; gaps. One figure.
2. Comparability: separate dissolved from total; reconcile units; identify the different reporting limits and method changes; do not combine incompatible definitions.
3. Concentration-discharge relationship per site and constituent: log concentration against log discharge with seasonal harmonics, fitted on the pre-period, producing flow-adjusted residuals. Show the C-Q plot pre and post for the headline cases.
4. Interrupted time series on the flow-adjusted series with a level change and a slope change at the release date, excluding a stated plume-passage window. Fit with the uncertainty structure the data have (autocorrelated residuals; use Newey-West or a simple AR(1) adjustment and say so). Treat non-detects as censored: a left-censored regression (Tobit-style) or a transparent bounds analysis, with substitution only as a labelled sensitivity check.
5. Sensitivity: vary the pre-period start (for example 2005, 2010, 2013), the post-period end, and the plume window; show the estimated level change under each. This grid is the headline figure.
6. A change-point search on the flow-adjusted residuals that does not know the release date (binary segmentation or PELT on the monthly series). Report where the detected change-points fall relative to the release and to the treatment-plant start. If the strongest change-point sits at the treatment plant, say so.
7. Use the upstream control site as a difference-in-differences style check for the sites that have one.
8. Multiplicity: several sites times several constituents. State how many comparisons were made and report accordingly; do not cherry-pick the one significant cell.

Report uncertainty appropriate to a seasonal, autocorrelated, unevenly sampled series. Avoid precision the data do not support. Describe change in measured concentrations; do not infer a health, ecological, or legal conclusion.

TESTS

Focused pytest tests: the C-Q regression recovers known slope and seasonal amplitude from a synthetic series; the interrupted time series recovers a planted level change and reports no change on a null series; censored regression on a fixture with known censoring; unit reconciliation; the join of samples to discharge does not multiply rows and handles missing days; the plume-window exclusion removes exactly the intended rows; the change-point search finds a planted step and nothing on white noise.
